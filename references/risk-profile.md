# Risk willingness questionnaire

Eight questions. Score each 0–3. Total 0–24.

Score what the person **has done**, not what they say they would do. Where a question asks about behaviour, prefer a verifiable past action over a hypothetical answer.

---

**Q1. Your portfolio falls 20% in three months and the news is uniformly bad. You:**
- 0 — Sell everything and wait for clarity
- 1 — Sell part of the equity to stop the bleeding
- 2 — Hold and change nothing
- 3 — Buy more, rebalancing back to target

**Q2. Largest single drawdown you have actually lived through without selling:**
- 0 — None; I have never held a losing position for long
- 1 — Under 10%
- 2 — 10–25%
- 3 — More than 25%

**Q3. If your portfolio fell 30% the year before you needed the money, you would:**
- 0 — Be unable to proceed with the plan at all
- 1 — Delay the goal by several years
- 2 — Delay by a year or two and adjust the amount
- 3 — Proceed as planned; that money was not needed at that date

**Q4. Which best describes your investing experience?**
- 0 — Savings accounts and term deposits only
- 1 — Funds, mostly chosen on recommendation
- 2 — I select funds and understand fees, duration, and diversification
- 3 — I have managed through at least one full market cycle, including a crash

**Q5. Choose one:**
- 0 — A certain 4% gain
- 1 — A 50/50 chance of 2% or 9%
- 2 — A 50/50 chance of −6% or 22%
- 3 — A 50/50 chance of −18% or 40%

**Q6. How much of this money could you lose without changing your lifestyle?**
- 0 — None of it; it is all committed
- 1 — Less than 10%
- 2 — 10–25%
- 3 — More than 25%

**Q7. Your portfolio gains 35% in a year while peers report 10%. You:**
- 0 — Sell it all; I do not trust gains I cannot explain
- 1 — Sell most and lock it in
- 2 — Trim back to target and rebalance
- 3 — Leave it; the plan says rebalance on schedule, not on feelings

**Q8. Which sentence do you agree with more?**
- 0 — "Avoiding loss matters more than earning; I would rather miss gains than lose capital."
- 1 — "I want stability first, with some growth."
- 2 — "I accept meaningful swings for higher long-run growth."
- 3 — "Volatility is the price of returns; I am comfortable with large swings."

---

## Scoring

| Total | Willingness level |
|---|---|
| ≥ 19 | 5 — Aggressive |
| 15–18 | 4 — Growth |
| 11–14 | 3 — Balanced |
| 7–10 | 2 — Conservative |
| ≤ 6 | 1 — Very Conservative |

**Then apply the lower-of-two rule** (`framework.md` §2.3): final level = min(capacity level, this level). Do not report the higher number as encouragement.

## Two interview notes

**Discount stated intentions.** If the person says "I can handle a 40% drop" but Q2 shows they have never held a 10% loss, trust Q2. The self-report is about identity; Q2 is about behaviour.

**Do not run this questionnaire before M1.** A household with three months of liquidity and 40% debt service has a capacity level of 1 regardless of what they answer here, and asking the questions first raises expectations that then have to be walked back.
